Many learners say: "I know English, but when I speak I translate from Hindi first, so I am slow." That is normal. Translation is slow because you build every sentence from the beginning. Thinking in English means you reach for **ready-made chunks** that are already in English. You can train this step by step. You do not need to be perfect and you do not need a teacher.

> **Hinglish mein:** Jab tum Hindi mein sochkar English mein translate karte ho, tab speed dheemi ho jaati hai. Solution: chhote English chunks yaad karo aur unhe baar-baar bolo, taaki unko Hindi se guzarna na pade.

## Why translation is slow

- Hindi and English have different word order, so word-for-word translation creates mistakes.
- You look for the "perfect" English word, and you stop speaking.
- You try to say a long, complex sentence instead of a short, simple one.

## Use chunks, not single words

A chunk is a group of words you learn and say together. You do not build it, you just say it.

| Instead of translating | Use the chunk |
| --- | --- |
| Mujhe check karne do. | Let me check. |
| Main aapko wapas batata hoon. | I'll get back to you. |
| Mujhe pakka nahi pata, lekin mujhe lagta hai... | I'm not sure, but I think... |
| Kya aap mujhe sun sakte hain? | Can you hear me? |
| Mujhe ek second chahiye. | Give me a second. |

## Seven habits to start thinking in English

1. **Name things.** Say the English word for objects around your desk: keyboard, charger, notebook.
2. **Narrate your day.** "I'm opening my laptop. I'm checking my email. I'm joining the stand-up."
3. **Use simple sentences.** If you do not know a hard word, say it in an easy way.
4. **Explain the missing word.** Forgot "laptop charger"? Say "the cable for my laptop".
5. **Answer in English in your head.** When somebody asks you something in any language, form the answer in English first.
6. **Describe your code.** Explain what a function does as if you were showing it to a teammate.
7. **Write three sentences a day** in a small notebook or notes app: what you did, what you learned, what you will do tomorrow.

## Talk around the word

Nobody knows every word. Use these phrases when you forget a word.

```phrases
It's the thing that shows the errors.
It's like a list, but you can't change it.
What I mean is, the page loads very slowly.
I can't remember the word, but it helps you save time.
Sorry, how do you say that in English?
```

## Practice: a five-minute narration

1. Set a timer for five minutes.
2. Speak aloud in English about what you are doing: where you are, what you see, what you will do next.
3. If you get stuck, use a simple sentence or talk around the word. Do not stop.
4. Do not look anything up while the timer runs. Write down new words and look them up afterwards.

## Mistakes are information

When you make a mistake, you have found the exact place to improve. Write the corrected sentence and say it three times. A learner who makes mistakes and keeps speaking improves faster than a learner who waits until it is perfect.

## Recap

- Translating word by word is slow. Use chunks you already know.
- Narrate your day, describe your code and write three sentences daily.
- If you forget a word, explain it with simple words and keep going.
- Mistakes show you what to practice next.
