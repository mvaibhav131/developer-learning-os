In meetings, calls and code reviews you will sometimes not understand what someone said. This is normal for everyone, including native speakers. The worst response is to nod and guess, because a wrong guess becomes a bug or a missed deadline. A polite question is a sign of a careful engineer. This lesson gives you the phrases and the method.

> **Hinglish mein:** Samajh na aaye to "haan haan" mat bolo. Politely poochho. "Could you repeat that?" ya "Just to confirm..." bolna professional lagta hai, kamzor nahi.

## Ask to repeat or slow down

| Situation | Say |
| --- | --- |
| You did not hear | Sorry, could you say that again, please? |
| Too fast | Could you speak a little more slowly? |
| You missed one part | I didn't catch the last part. Could you repeat it? |
| Bad audio | Sorry, the audio is breaking up. Could you repeat that? |
| You want text | Could you write that in the chat, please? |

## Ask for a meaning or an example

| Situation | Say |
| --- | --- |
| Unknown word or term | What does "idempotent" mean in this case? |
| Too abstract | Could you give me an example? |
| Unclear plan | Could you walk me through the steps? |
| You want a visual | Could you share your screen and show me? |

## Confirm what you understood

This is the most useful technique. **Repeat the message in your own words** and ask if it is right.

- Just to make sure I understand, you want me to change the API response, and keep the database the same. Is that right?
- So, the deadline is Friday, and the demo is on Monday. Did I get that right?
- Let me repeat that to confirm: I will update the tests first, and then you will review.

## Check which option they mean

- Do you mean the old API or the new API?
- Are you asking about the staging server or production?
- When you say "soon", do you mean today or this week?

## Close the loop

After you receive the answer, tell them that you now understand, and write the next steps.

```phrases
Sorry, could you say that again, please?
I didn't catch the last part. Could you repeat it?
Could you give me an example?
Just to make sure I understand, you want me to update the API. Is that right?
Do you mean the staging server or production?
Thanks, that's clear now.
So the next steps are: I update the tests, and you review by Thursday.
```

## Tips

1. Ask early. A question in the first minute is easier than a confusion at the end.
2. Ask **one** question at a time.
3. Ask in the chat if you are shy to speak. It is also useful when the audio is bad.
4. End each meeting by writing the action items in the team channel. It protects you and the team.

## Common mistakes

| Less helpful | Better |
| --- | --- |
| What? | Sorry, could you say that again? |
| I don't understand. | I'm not sure I understood. Could you explain it another way? |
| Say again. | Could you repeat that, please? |
| Yes yes. (but you did not understand) | Just to confirm, you mean... |

## Practice

1. Watch a short stand-up or meeting video from this lesson. Pause after each speaker.
2. Write one clarification question for each speaker, using a phrase from the lists above.
3. Practice the confirm technique with a friend or a recording: say "Just to make sure I understand, you want me to..." and complete it in your own words.

## Recap

- Do not nod and guess. Ask politely.
- Use phrases: could you say that again, I didn't catch the last part, could you give me an example.
- Confirm in your own words: Just to make sure I understand...
- Finish with next steps in writing.
