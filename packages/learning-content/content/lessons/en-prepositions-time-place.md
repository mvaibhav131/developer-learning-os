Hindi uses small words after a noun ("mein", "par", "se"), and one Hindi word often becomes several English words. That is why **in, on and at** feel random. The trick is to remember a picture for each word, and then learn a small number of fixed phrases.

> **Hinglish mein:** "Mein / par / pe" ke liye English mein in, on, at alag-alag situations mein aate hain. Pehle chhote pictures yaad karo: at = ek point, on = surface ya din, in = andar ya lamba samay.

## Prepositions of time

| Word | Picture | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| at | an exact point in time | at 10 am, at noon, at night, at the moment, at the weekend |
| on | days and dates | on Monday, on 5 May, on my birthday, on Friday morning |
| in | longer periods | in May, in 2025, in the morning, in the evening, in summer, in two weeks |

Remember: **in the morning** but **on Monday morning**. **At night**, but **in the evening**.

## Prepositions of place

| Word | Picture | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| at | a point or an address | at the office, at home, at work, at the airport, at my desk, at a meeting |
| in | inside a space | in the room, in Pune, in India, in the repo, in the database, in the cloud |
| on | a surface or a line | on the table, on the screen, on GitHub, on the server, on Slack, on the main branch |

## Tech examples

- The bug is **in** the login module.
- The code is **on** GitHub.
- The release is **on** Friday.
- We deploy **at** midnight.
- Please send the report **by** Friday. (not later than Friday)
- I will work **until** 6 pm. (continues up to 6 pm)
- You will receive the invoice **within** two days.
- I will be ready **in** ten minutes. (ten minutes from now)

## Movement prepositions

| Word | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| to | destination | I'm going to the office. |
| into | moving inside | Copy the file into the folder. |
| from | starting point | I work from home. |
| through | from one side to the other | The request passes through the load balancer. |

Do not use a preposition after **reach, enter, discuss or attend**: reach the office, enter the room, discuss the plan, attend the meeting.

## Preposition + adjective or verb

Some prepositions go with fixed words. Learn them as pairs.

| Wrong | Correct |
| --- | --- |
| good in Java | good at Java |
| interested for AI | interested in AI |
| depends of the cache | depends on the cache |
| afraid from failure | afraid of failure |
| angry on him | angry with him |
| married with Rahul | married to Rahul |
| waiting for the build | waiting for the build |

## Say them aloud

```phrases
The bug is in the login module.
Please send me the report by Friday.
We deploy to production on Friday at six.
I will be at my desk until five.
The code is on GitHub, in the main branch.
I'm interested in backend development.
```

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Correct |
| --- | --- |
| I'll meet you in Monday. | I'll meet you on Monday. |
| I was born at 1999. | I was born in 1999. |
| I live at Pune. | I live in Pune. |
| I'm going in the office. | I'm going to the office. |
| Meeting is on 10 am. | The meeting is at 10 am. |

## Practice

Fill in in, on, at, by, until or to.

1. The standup starts ___ 10 am. Answer: at
2. I was born ___ March. Answer: in
3. The deadline is ___ Friday. Answer: on
4. Please reply ___ tomorrow. (not later than) Answer: by
5. I'm going ___ the airport. Answer: to

## Recap

- At = a point, on = days, dates and surfaces, in = months, years and inside places.
- By = not later than. Until = up to a time.
- Reach, enter, discuss, attend: no preposition.
- Learn the fixed pairs: good at, interested in, depends on, afraid of.
