At the end of almost every interview you hear: "Do you have any questions for us?" The worst answer is "No, nothing." It looks like you are not curious. Good questions show that you have researched the company, that you think about the work and that you want to do it well. They also help **you** decide if this is the right job. You should prepare three to five questions before every interview.

> **Hinglish mein:** Interview ke end mein "No questions" mat bolo. 3-5 sawaal pehle se taiyaar rakho: team, kaam, seekhne ke mauke, aur agle steps ke baare mein. Isse pata chalta hai ki tumhe job mein sach mein interest hai.

## Good questions by topic

| Topic | Question |
| --- | --- |
| The work | What would a typical day or week look like in this role? |
| The team | How is the team organised, and who would I work with most? |
| Success | What would success look like in the first three months? |
| Growth | What learning opportunities are there for developers here? |
| Technology | What does your tech stack look like, and what are the biggest technical challenges? |
| Process | How do you handle code reviews, testing and releases? |
| Culture | What do you like most about working here? |
| Next steps | What are the next steps in the process, and when can I expect to hear from you? |

## Questions to avoid

| Avoid | Why | Better |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What does your company do? | It shows you did not research. | I read that you build X. How does this team contribute to it? |
| How much salary will I get? (first) | Too early, and it can seem like the only interest. | (Ask later, when they bring it up.) |
| How many holidays will I get? | It can wait until the offer stage. | What is the working style of the team? |
| Do I get promoted soon? | It sounds like an expectation. | What does career growth look like here? |
| Any yes or no question | It ends the conversation. | Use "How", "What", "Could you tell me about...". |

## Phrases for the end of the interview

```phrases
Thank you for your time. I enjoyed learning about the team.
What would success look like in the first three months?
Could you tell me more about the tech stack and the biggest challenges?
How does the team handle code reviews and testing?
What are the next steps in the process?
I'm very interested in this role. Thank you again.
```

## Listen to the answer

Ask a follow-up question when you hear something interesting: "That's interesting. How does that work in practice?" This shows that you listen.

## Thank-you email

Send a short email within 24 hours of the interview.

```text
Subject: Thank you: Backend Developer interview

Hi Ms. Rao,

Thank you for taking the time to talk with me today. I enjoyed learning
about the payment platform and the way your team handles code reviews.

I'm very interested in the role, and I think my experience with Java and
APIs would be useful for the team. Please let me know if you need any
other information.

Thank you again,
Rahul Verma
```

## Practice

1. Choose a company you want to work for. Read its website and one article about it.
2. Write five questions: two about the work, one about the team, one about growth, one about the next steps.
3. Say them aloud so that they feel natural.
4. Write your thank-you email for a real or imaginary interview.

## Recap

- Always have three to five questions ready.
- Ask about the work, team, success, growth and next steps.
- Do not ask what the company does, and do not start with salary or holidays.
- Send a short thank-you email within a day.
