Small talk is a short, light conversation about easy topics. It looks unimportant, but it builds trust. People who talk for a minute before a meeting, at the coffee machine or on a video call work better together. If you are shy about your English, small talk is also the safest speaking practice, because the topics are simple and the stakes are low.

> **Hinglish mein:** Small talk ka matlab hai chhoti, halki baatein: weekend, khana, mausam, kaam. Ek rule yaad rakho: jawab do aur wapas sawaal poochho ("Pretty good, thanks. And you?"). Isse baat chalti rehti hai.

## The ping-pong rule

Small talk is like table tennis. Answer in a full sentence, add a small detail, and then **ask the same question back**.

| Person A | Person B |
| --- | --- |
| How's your day going? | Pretty good, thanks. A bit busy with the release. How about you? |
| Did you have a good weekend? | Yes, I visited my parents. And you? |

One-word answers ("fine", "yes") end the conversation. Add one more sentence.

## Safe topics

| Safe | Avoid at work |
| --- | --- |
| weekend and plans, food, travel, hobbies, films, sports, weather, tech news, the commute | salary, religion, politics, age, marriage, health problems, gossip about colleagues |

## Starters

| Situation | Say |
| --- | --- |
| Morning | Good morning! How's your day going? |
| Monday | How was your weekend? |
| Friday | Any plans for the weekend? |
| Before a call starts | Hi everyone. Can you all hear me? |
| In the kitchen | How's the project going? |
| After a holiday | Welcome back! How was your trip? |

## Answers that keep it going

```phrases
Pretty good, thanks. How about you?
Busy, but good. We have a release on Friday.
I went to see a film. Have you seen anything good lately?
Not much. I just relaxed at home. What about you?
That sounds nice. Where did you go?
I'm trying a new coffee place near the office. Do you know any good ones?
```

## Join and leave politely

| Situation | Say |
| --- | --- |
| Joining a group | Do you mind if I join you? |
| Ending the chat | It was nice talking to you. |
| You need to go | I should get back to work. Let's catch up later. |
| Before a meeting | I think we're about to start. Talk to you later. |

## Small talk on video calls

- Join one or two minutes early and say hello to the first people.
- Use the chat when you cannot find the right words.
- Keep it short: one or two exchanges, then start the meeting.

## Common mistakes

| Less helpful | Better |
| --- | --- |
| Fine. | Pretty good, thanks. How about you? |
| What is your salary? | (Do not ask.) How do you like the new office? |
| You look tired. | Busy week? |
| What is your good name? | I'm Priya. What's your name? |

## Practice

1. Prepare three open questions, such as "How was your weekend?", "Have you seen anything good lately?" and "How's the project going?".
2. Use one of them with a real colleague today.
3. Practice the ping-pong rule: answer, add a detail, ask back.
4. Write down one new phrase you heard and use it tomorrow.

## Recap

- Small talk builds trust, and it is easy speaking practice.
- Answer with a full sentence, add a detail and ask the same question back.
- Choose safe topics: weekend, food, travel, hobbies, tech.
- End the talk politely: "It was nice talking to you."
