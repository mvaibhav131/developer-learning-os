An idiom is a phrase whose meaning is different from its words. "Touch base" has nothing to do with a base or with touching. If you hear idioms in a meeting and you do not know them, you may feel lost. If you understand them, you also understand the jokes and the culture of the team. You do not have to use idioms to speak well. **First learn to understand them. Use only a few, and only the ones you are sure about.**

> **Hinglish mein:** Idiom ka matlab uske shabdon se alag hota hai. "The ball is in your court" ka matlab hai "ab faisla tumhare haath mein hai". Pehle samajhna seekho, phir kuch hi idioms bolo, aur sirf tab jab pakka pata ho.

## Idioms for meetings and planning

| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| touch base | contact someone briefly | Let's touch base on Monday. |
| on the same page | agree and understand the same thing | Let's make sure we're on the same page about the deadline. |
| keep me in the loop | keep me informed | Please keep me in the loop about the release. |
| heads-up | an early warning | Just a heads-up: the server will be down at midnight. |
| circle back | return to a topic later | Let's circle back to this after the demo. |
| ballpark figure | a rough number | What's a ballpark figure for the cost? |
| get the ball rolling | start something | Let's get the ball rolling with the first task. |
| the ball is in your court | it is your turn to act or decide | I've sent the proposal, so the ball is in your court. |

## Idioms for problems and decisions

| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| back to square one | start again from the beginning | The design failed, so we're back to square one. |
| hit a wall | be unable to continue | I hit a wall while debugging the cache. |
| red flag | a warning sign | A missing test suite is a red flag. |
| bite the bullet | do something difficult that you cannot avoid | We decided to bite the bullet and rewrite the module. |
| cut corners | do something quickly and not carefully | We can't cut corners with security. |
| reinvent the wheel | do again what already exists | Don't reinvent the wheel. Use the library. |
| tip of the iceberg | a small part of a bigger problem | These errors are just the tip of the iceberg. |
| silver bullet | a simple answer to a hard problem | There is no silver bullet for performance. |

## Idioms for work and effort

| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| low-hanging fruit | easy tasks with good results | Let's fix the low-hanging fruit first. |
| hit the ground running | start working effectively from day one | She hit the ground running in her new job. |
| learning curve | the time needed to learn something | Kubernetes has a steep learning curve. |
| burn the midnight oil | work late into the night | We burned the midnight oil to finish the release. |
| run out of steam | lose energy | The team ran out of steam before the deadline. |
| a piece of cake | very easy | The migration was a piece of cake. |
| game changer | something that changes everything | Automated testing was a game changer for us. |
| rule of thumb | a practical, not exact, rule | As a rule of thumb, keep functions short. |

## Say them aloud

```phrases
Let's touch base on Monday to check the progress.
I want to make sure we're on the same page.
Just a heads-up: the server will be down at midnight.
Let's circle back to this after the demo.
Please keep me in the loop about the release.
There is no silver bullet for performance problems.
```

## Use idioms carefully

1. **Understand first.** Learn to recognise the idioms above when you hear them.
2. **Use a few.** Two or three that you really understand are enough.
3. **Check the context.** Some idioms are informal and are not right for a formal email.
4. **Different countries use different idioms.** Do not use idioms in a presentation for a global audience without being sure.
5. **Do not change the words.** "On the same book" is not the same idiom as "on the same page".

## Practice

1. Choose five idioms from the tables above and write a work sentence for each.
2. Listen for idioms in the next meeting or video. Write down the ones you hear.
3. Use "heads-up" or "keep me in the loop" in a real message this week.

## Recap

- Idioms have meanings that you cannot guess from the words.
- Understand many, use only a few.
- Learn idioms in whole phrases and sentences.
- Check whether the context is formal or informal.
