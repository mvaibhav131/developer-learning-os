Most people start learning English with a lot of energy, and stop after two weeks. The reason is not laziness. The plan was too big, too vague, or it depended on feeling motivated. Motivation goes up and down. A good habit works on a bad day too. This lesson gives you a small, specific plan and a way to continue when life gets busy.

> **Hinglish mein:** Motivation hamesha nahi rehti. Isliye plan chhota rakho aur ek fixed time se jodo: "Chai banane ke baad 10 minute English". Roz karna hai, perfect karna zaroori nahi.

## Principles of a lasting habit

1. **Make it tiny.** Ten minutes is better than a big plan that you skip.
2. **Make it specific.** Not "practise English", but "shadow one video for ten minutes".
3. **Tie it to a trigger.** After something you already do every day: "After I make tea, I practise English."
4. **Track it.** A visible record keeps you going.
5. **Never skip twice.** One bad day is normal. Two in a row makes a new habit of skipping.
6. **Know your reason.** Write one sentence: "I'm learning English because I want to..."

## Habit stacking: choose a trigger

| Trigger | New habit |
| --- | --- |
| After I make my morning tea | Read aloud five practice phrases |
| On my commute | Listen to one English clip |
| Before my stand-up | Say my update aloud once |
| After dinner | Write three sentences in my journal |
| Before I sleep | Review five new words |

## A four-week starter plan

| Week | Focus | Daily time | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Listening | 10 min | One short clip with three passes. Five new phrases. |
| 2 | Listening and speaking | 15 min | Listening for 8 minutes, then speak aloud for 7 minutes (shadowing). |
| 3 | Add writing | 20 min | Add three sentences about your day, or a message at work in English. |
| 4 | Use it at work | 20 min | Say your stand-up update in English. Write one email in English. Record a mock interview answer. |

After week four, continue and make one part harder: a longer clip, a new topic or a real conversation.

## Set up your space

- Change your phone language to English, or at least your apps.
- Follow a few English tech channels instead of Hindi entertainment channels during your practice time.
- Keep a small notebook (or a note) for new phrases.
- Put your headphones where you can see them.

## Tracking in this app

- Time on lessons is logged automatically as study time.
- In **Settings**, you can set a daily goal for English, for example 15 minutes. The dashboard then shows your progress.
- Only count real practice, not music or a video playing in the background.
- Look at your streak weekly, not hourly. A weekly look shows the trend.

## When you have a bad day

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Very busy | Do the five-minute version: read aloud five phrases. |
| Tired | Do the easy version: listen to one clip. |
| You missed yesterday | Do a short session today. Do not "catch up" with two hours. |
| You feel stuck | Go back to an easier clip. Progress is not a straight line. |

## Phrases to say to yourself

```phrases
I'm learning a little every day.
One short session is better than none.
I make mistakes, and that's how I improve.
Today I will practise for ten minutes.
I did it again today. I'm building a habit.
```

## Honest expectations

- No lesson can promise fluency in 30 days. Progress depends on how much you practise.
- You will have good days and bad days. Keep going.
- Real progress is visible after months, but you can feel small improvements in weeks.

## Recap

- Tiny, specific, tied to a trigger, and tracked.
- Never skip twice. Do the five-minute version on bad days.
- Follow the four-week plan, then make one part harder.
- Use English at work every day. That is real practice.
