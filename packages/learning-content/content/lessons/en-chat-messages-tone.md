Team chat (Slack, Microsoft Teams or similar) is where most of your daily English happens. Messages are short, quick and informal, but they still need to be clear. The biggest mistake is sending "Hi" and waiting for a reply. The other person has to answer "Hi, what's up?", and you have lost ten minutes. A good message can be answered in one reply, even when the other person is in a meeting.

> **Hinglish mein:** Chat mein sirf "Hi" ya "Are you there?" mat bhejo. Ek hi message mein pura sawaal likho: context, sawaal, tumne kya try kiya, aur kab tak chahiye. Isse jawab jaldi milta hai.

## The four-part message

| Part | What to write | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Context | What you are working on | I'm testing the payment page on staging. |
| Question | One clear question | The page shows a 500 error after login. Do you know if the API changed? |
| What you tried | Shows you did your part | I restarted the server and checked the logs. |
| Deadline | When you need it | I need to know by 3 pm for the demo. |

## Bad and good

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| Hi | Hi Anita, quick question about the staging deploy: do you know why the login page shows a 500 error? I checked the logs, and it seems related to the API key. |
| Are you free? | Do you have five minutes today to look at my pull request? It is a small change. |
| It's not working. | The build fails on the main branch with a timeout in the test step. Here is the log: <link>. |
| Please check | Could you check the config file? I think the database URL is missing. |

## Tone

- Say **please** and **thanks**. Do not overdo it.
- Do not write in **ALL CAPITALS**. It looks like shouting.
- Use **thanks** after a reply, even a short one.
- Use thread replies, so that the main channel stays clean.
- Use @name when you need a specific person, not @channel for small questions.

## Common work abbreviations

| Abbreviation | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| FYI | for your information |
| ASAP | as soon as possible (use with care, it can sound pushy) |
| EOD | end of day |
| ETA | estimated time of arrival: when you expect to finish |
| OOO | out of office |
| WFH | working from home |
| LGTM | looks good to me |
| TL;DR | too long; didn't read: a short summary |
| AFAIK | as far as I know |
| IMO | in my opinion |

## Useful messages

```phrases
Hi Anita, quick question about the staging deploy.
I'm looking into this now. I'll update you in thirty minutes.
Thanks, that helped. It works now.
FYI, the release moved to Friday.
Could you take a look at this when you have a minute?
I'll be offline from three to four. Message me if it's urgent.
```

## Urgent or not?

- **Urgent:** production is down, data is at risk. Use a call or a direct message, and say clearly "This is urgent."
- **Normal:** most questions. Write the full message, and wait for a reply.
- **Low:** ideas, FYIs. Post in the channel and do not expect an immediate answer.

## Practice

1. Rewrite "Hi, are you there? I have a problem" as a four-part message with real details from your work.
2. Write a short update for your team: what you are working on, what is next and one blocker.
3. Replace one abbreviation you do not know with its meaning, and use it once this week.

## Recap

- Write one complete message: context, question, what you tried, deadline.
- No "Hi" alone and no ALL CAPS.
- Say please, thanks and use threads.
- Know the common abbreviations, but write clearly first.
