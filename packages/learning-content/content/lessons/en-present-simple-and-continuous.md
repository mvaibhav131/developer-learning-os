Two tenses cover most of what you say in a daily stand-up. The **present simple** tells what usually happens. The **present continuous** tells what is happening now or around now. Choosing between them correctly makes your updates sound natural.

> **Hinglish mein:** Present simple = aadat, facts, schedule ("Main roz 9 baje start karta hoon"). Present continuous = abhi chal raha kaam ("Main abhi login bug debug kar raha hoon"). Hindi mein dono ke liye "hoon / hai" lagta hai, isliye English mein confusion hota hai.

## Present simple

Use it for habits, facts, routines and timetables.

- I **start** work at 9:30. (habit)
- The build **takes** ten minutes. (fact)
- Our sprint **ends** on Friday. (schedule)

Form: base verb. For **he, she, it** add **-s** or **-es**: She writes tests. It works. He fixes bugs. After sh, ch, s, x, o: add -es (push -> pushes, catch -> catches, go -> goes). After consonant + y: change y to ies (study -> studies). Negative: **do not / does not** + base verb. Question: **Do / Does** + subject + base verb.

## Present continuous

Use it for things happening at this moment or in this period, and for plans already fixed.

- I **am debugging** the login flow right now.
- We **are migrating** to a new database this month.
- She **is meeting** the client tomorrow. (arranged plan)

Form: **am / is / are + verb-ing**. Spelling: code -> coding (drop the e), run -> running (double the last letter), deploy -> deploying, debug -> debugging, test -> testing.

## Time words that help you choose

| Present simple | Present continuous |
| --- | --- |
| always, usually, often, sometimes, never | now, right now, at the moment |
| every day, every week, on Mondays | today, this week, this month, currently |
| once a week, twice a day | these days, still |

## Verbs that do not usually use -ing

Some verbs describe states, not actions. Use the present simple with them, even for "now".

| Wrong | Correct |
| --- | --- |
| I am knowing the answer. | I know the answer. |
| I am understanding the task. | I understand the task. |
| She is wanting a new laptop. | She wants a new laptop. |
| We are needing more time. | We need more time. |
| He is having two monitors. | He has two monitors. |

Other stative verbs: like, love, hate, prefer, believe, remember, mean, own, belong, seem.

## Using both in a stand-up

```phrases
I usually start my day by checking the logs.
Today I am working on the payment API.
We are waiting for the design team to finish the screens.
She is testing the new release this afternoon.
The deployment takes about fifteen minutes.
I understand the problem, and I am fixing it now.
```

## Common mistakes

| Wrong | Correct |
| --- | --- |
| She work in Pune. | She works in Pune. |
| Every day I am using VS Code. | Every day I use VS Code. |
| I am go to office by metro. | I go to the office by metro. |
| Now I work on the bug. | Now I am working on the bug. |
| Does she works here? | Does she work here? |

## Practice

Choose the correct form: present simple or present continuous.

1. Our team (have) a stand-up every morning at 10.
2. Right now I (look) at the error log.
3. He usually (not eat) lunch at his desk.
4. They (learn) Kubernetes this month.

Answers: 1. has. 2. am looking. 3. does not eat. 4. are learning.

Then write three sentences about your usual day and three sentences about what you are doing at this moment. Read them aloud.

## Recap

- Present simple: habits, facts, schedules. Add -s for he, she, it.
- Present continuous: now, this period, fixed plans. Use am, is or are + -ing.
- Do not use -ing with know, understand, want, need, like, have (for owning).
- Time words help you choose: usually and every day for simple, now and currently for continuous.
