Behavioural questions begin with "Tell me about a time when..." or "Give me an example of...". The interviewer wants proof from real life: how you handle conflict, pressure, mistakes and teamwork. If you give a general answer ("I am a good team player"), they learn nothing. If you tell a short story with a clear structure, they see how you act. The **STAR** method gives you that structure.

> **Hinglish mein:** STAR = Situation (kya hua), Task (tumhari zimmedari), Action (tumne kya kiya), Result (nateeja). Ek short story banao, 1-2 minute mein, aur "I" se batao ki tumne kya kiya.

## The four parts

| Letter | Part | What to say | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S | Situation | The background in one or two sentences | 15 seconds |
| T | Task | Your goal or responsibility | 10 seconds |
| A | Action | What **you** did, step by step. This is the longest part. | 45 seconds |
| R | Result | The outcome, with a number if you have one. What you learned. | 20 seconds |

## Sample: a deadline problem

> **Situation:** Last year our team had to release a payment feature in two weeks, but a teammate left suddenly. **Task:** I had to take over his part, which was the refund API, and still finish my own work. **Action:** First, I listed all the tasks and asked my manager to move one low-priority task to the next sprint. Then I read my teammate's code, asked the QA engineer which cases were most important, and wrote the tests first. I also gave a short update to the team every day. **Result:** We released on time, and the refund API had no critical bugs in the first month. I learned to ask for priorities early.

Use your own real stories.

## Story ideas to prepare

| Question | Your story |
| --- | --- |
| A time you disagreed with someone | A technical disagreement you solved with data |
| A time you failed or made a mistake | A bug in production, and what you changed afterwards |
| A tight deadline | The sample above |
| A time you took the lead | You suggested and finished an improvement |
| A time you learned something new quickly | A new framework you learned for a project |

Prepare **five** stories. Each one can answer several questions.

## Phrases for each part

```phrases
The situation was that we had two weeks to release a payment feature.
My task was to take over the refund API.
First, I listed all the tasks and asked my manager about the priorities.
Then I wrote the tests before I changed the code.
As a result, we released on time with no critical bugs.
What I learned is that it helps to ask for priorities early.
```

## Tips

1. Say **I**, not only we. The interviewer is hiring you.
2. Put most of your time into the **Action**.
3. Add a number or a clear result: "reduced from 8 seconds to 2 seconds".
4. In a failure story, add a **learning**: "Since then, I always...".
5. Do not blame others. Describe what you did.
6. Keep each story under two minutes.

## Common mistakes

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| We did everything together. | I wrote the API and my teammate wrote the UI. |
| It was a big problem. | The page took eight seconds to load. |
| Everything went fine. | We released on time, and users reported no crashes. |

## Practice

1. Choose one story from the list above.
2. Write it as four short paragraphs: S, T, A, R.
3. Say it aloud and time it. Cut it to two minutes.
4. Record, listen and fix one thing. Repeat for another story.

## Recap

- STAR: situation, task, action, result.
- Action is the longest part. Say I.
- Add a number or a clear result and one learning.
- Prepare five true stories. Do not invent.
