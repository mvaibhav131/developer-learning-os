You do not need to lose your Indian accent to be understood. You need to be **clear**. A few sound changes cause most misunderstandings, and they are easy to practice for five minutes a day: **v and w, the two th sounds, short and long vowels, and the endings -ed and -s.**

> **Hinglish mein:** Accent hatane ki zaroorat nahi hai. Bas kuch sounds saaf karne hain, jaise v aur w ka fark, "th", chhote-bade vowels aur -ed / -s ke ending. Roz paanch minute practice kaafi hai.

## V and W

In Hindi, one letter covers both sounds. In English they are different.

| Sound | How to make it | Words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **v** | Top teeth touch your lower lip. Use your voice. | very, version, value, variable, server, review, validate, event |
| **w** | Round your lips like "oo". Teeth do not touch the lip. | we, web, work, window, workflow, software, hardware, firewall |

Minimal pairs (they differ by only one sound): vest and west, vine and wine, vet and wet, verse and worse, very and wary.

## The two TH sounds

English has two "th" sounds. Both need your tongue **lightly between your teeth**.

| Sound | Voice | Words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unvoiced th (like blowing air) | No voice. Do not make your throat vibrate. | think, three, thread, method, month, authentication, path |
| Voiced th | Voice on. Feel a buzz in your throat. | this, that, the, they, there, with, other |

Common problem: saying "tink" for think, "dis" for this. Put the tongue tip between the teeth and let air flow. Do not stop the air with the tongue.

## Short and long vowels

Vowel length changes the word. Make long vowels really long and short vowels really short.

| Short | Long |
| --- | --- |
| ship | sheep |
| bit | beat |
| full | fool |
| pull | pool |
| live (verb) | leave |

Also practice **cat / cut / cot**, **bad / bed**, and **hot / hat**.

## The ending -ed

The ending **-ed** has three sounds. It is rarely a full "ed".

| Sound | After | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **t** | p, k, f, s, sh, ch | worked, pushed, fixed, stopped, matched |
| **d** | most other sounds | deployed, merged, saved, called, played |
| **id** | t or d | tested, added, committed, needed |

## The ending -s

| Sound | After | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **s** | p, t, k, f | commits, tests, blocks |
| **z** | most other sounds | bugs, users, runs, servers, methods |
| **iz** | s, z, sh, ch, j | fixes, pushes, branches, changes, pages |

## Practice sentences

```phrases
The very fast server works well with the new web version.
Thank you for the three methods you shared this month.
We reviewed the workflow and validated every variable.
She pushed the fix, merged the branch and tested the build.
They think this thread is with the other team.
I deployed the software to the server on Wednesday.
```

## How to practice

1. Pick **one** sound per day. Do not try to fix all sounds at once.
2. Watch the lesson videos with the mouth shape in mind. Pause and copy it.
3. Use the record button on the sentences above. Listen to yourself, then listen to a video and compare.
4. Say each minimal pair five times, slowly and then at normal speed.
5. Use the sound in real conversation once today, for example the word "version" in your stand-up.

## Recap

- V uses teeth on lip. W uses round lips with no teeth.
- Th: tongue lightly between the teeth. Unvoiced for think, voiced for this.
- Keep short and long vowels different: ship and sheep.
- -ed can sound like t, d or id. -s can sound like s, z or iz.
- Aim to be clear, not to sound like someone else.
