At work you ask questions all day: "Did the tests pass?", "Does this change affect the API?", "Why is the build failing?" English uses small helping verbs, **do, does and did**, to build questions and negatives. Hindi does not have them, so they feel strange at first. Once the pattern is clear, it works everywhere.

> **Hinglish mein:** Hindi mein sawaal ke liye aksar sirf "kya" laga dete hain. English mein sawaal banane ke liye helping verb (do / does / did) subject se pehle aata hai, aur main verb apne simple form mein rehta hai.

## The core pattern

| Time | Question | Negative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present, I / you / we / they | **Do** you use TypeScript? | I **do not** (don't) use TypeScript. |
| Present, he / she / it | **Does** she use TypeScript? | She **does not** (doesn't) use TypeScript. |
| Past, all subjects | **Did** you push the code? | I **did not** (didn't) push the code. |

The most important rule: after **do, does or did**, the main verb stays in its **base form**. The helper already carries the tense and the "-s".

| Wrong | Correct |
| --- | --- |
| Does he works here? | Does he work here? |
| She doesn't knows the answer. | She doesn't know the answer. |
| Did you pushed the code? | Did you push the code? |
| I didn't went to the meeting. | I didn't go to the meeting. |

## Questions with "be" and modal verbs

Do not add do, does or did when the sentence already has **am, is, are, was, were** or a modal verb such as **can, could, should, will**. Just swap the order.

- You are free now. -> **Are you** free now?
- The build is green. -> **Is the build** green?
- You can review this. -> **Can you** review this?
- It was working yesterday. -> **Was it** working yesterday?

For negatives, add **not** after the helper: The build is not green. I cannot join the call. I will not be late.

## Question words (what, where, why, how)

Use: **question word + helper + subject + base verb**.

| Question word | Example |
| --- | --- |
| What | What does this function return? |
| Where | Where did you find the bug? |
| Why | Why do we need a cache? |
| How | How long does the deployment take? |
| When | When did the error start? |

Careful with this pattern: **Where you are going?** is wrong. Say **Where are you going?**

When the question word is the **subject** itself, there is no helper: **Who broke the build?** What happened? Which test failed?

## Short answers

You do not have to repeat the whole sentence. Repeat the helper.

- Did you test it? **Yes, I did.** / **No, I didn't.**
- Does it work on Windows? **Yes, it does.** / **No, it doesn't.**
- Are you joining the call? **Yes, I am.** / **No, I'm not.**

## Questions you can use in your team

```phrases
Did the tests pass?
Does this change affect the API?
Do we need approval for this?
How long does it take to run?
Why did the build fail?
Who owns this service?
Is the staging environment ready?
```

## Practice

Turn each statement into a question and a negative sentence.

1. She uses Docker. -> Does she use Docker? / She doesn't use Docker.
2. They deployed on Friday. -> Did they deploy on Friday? / They didn't deploy on Friday.
3. You are ready for the demo. -> Are you ready for the demo? / You are not ready for the demo.

Now do the same for three sentences about your own work. Say them aloud, with a clear pause before the question word.

## Recap

- Use do or does in the present and did in the past.
- After do, does or did, the main verb is in the base form: Does he **work**? Did you **push**?
- With be and modals, swap the order instead. No helper is needed.
- Question words come first: What does it return? Why did it fail?
- Short answers repeat the helper: Yes, I did. No, it doesn't.
