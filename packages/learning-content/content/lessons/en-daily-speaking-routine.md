The best English plan is the one you follow tomorrow, and the day after. Long plans fail because they need too much time. This routine takes **15 minutes**, needs only your phone or laptop, and covers the four things that matter: warming up your mouth, copying native speech, speaking in your own words, and learning from your mistakes.

> **Hinglish mein:** Roz sirf 15 minute. Warm-up, shadowing, ek answer record karna, aur review. Lamba plan banane se behtar hai chhota plan jo tum roz kar sako.

## The 15-minute routine

| Minutes | Step | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 3 | Warm-up | Read aloud five practice phrases from any lesson. Slowly first, then at normal speed. |
| 3 to 8 | Shadowing | Repeat a one-minute clip right after the speaker. Use the same clip for five days. |
| 8 to 12 | Speak and record | Answer one question for 60 to 90 seconds in your own words. Record it. |
| 12 to 15 | Review | Listen once. Write down two mistakes and the corrected sentences. Say each corrected sentence three times. |

## Weekly topics for the speaking step

| Day | Topic |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | Give your stand-up update: yesterday, today, blockers |
| Tuesday | Explain a bug you fixed or a bug you are working on |
| Wednesday | Describe one project in four sentences |
| Thursday | Answer one interview question, for example "Tell me about yourself" |
| Friday | Small talk: your weekend plans, a hobby, a film |
| Saturday | Free talk: record about anything, then listen to the last six recordings |
| Sunday | Rest, or only listen to an English video or podcast |

## Rules that keep it going

1. **Same time every day.** For example, right after your morning tea or before dinner.
2. **Never skip two days in a row.** If you miss one day, do five minutes the next day.
3. **Keep it small.** Fifteen minutes is enough. More is a bonus.
4. **Keep one notebook** with corrected sentences, new words and phrases.
5. **Check your progress weekly,** not daily. Listen to a recording from last week and compare.

## Tracking in this app

- Time you spend on lessons is logged automatically as a study session, so you can see it on the dashboard.
- In **Settings** you can set a daily goal for English, for example 15 minutes. The dashboard then shows progress from the sessions you really studied.
- Mark a lesson as complete only when you have done the practice, not just read it.

## A practice question to start with

```phrases
Tell me about your day so far.
What did you work on yesterday?
What is the most interesting thing you learned this week?
Describe your desk and your workspace.
What are you going to do tomorrow?
```

Choose one question. Set a timer for 90 seconds, record your answer, and listen to it once.

## Honest expectations

- A habit grows slowly. You may not notice a difference in a week, but you will notice it in a few months of regular practice.
- Recording yourself feels uncomfortable at first. That discomfort is a sign that you are practising the right thing.
- Mix the routine with reading and listening in your free time.

## Recap

- Warm up, shadow, speak and record, review. Fifteen minutes in total.
- Use a weekly topic list, so you never have to think about what to say.
- Never skip two days in a row. Small and regular wins.
- Check your progress in the app, and track real study time only.
