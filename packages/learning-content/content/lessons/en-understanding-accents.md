English is spoken by people from many countries, and each group has its own accent. In a global team you may work with colleagues from the US, the UK, Australia, Singapore, Ireland, Nigeria, Germany or Brazil, and everybody speaks a little differently. Understanding different accents is a skill that you can train. Two things make it hard: **different vowel and consonant sounds**, and **fast, connected speech** where words join together.

> **Hinglish mein:** Alag desh ke log English alag tareeke se bolte hain. Ghabrao mat. Pehle ek hi video ko alag-alag accent mein suno, aur seekho ki tez bolte waqt words aapas mein jud jaate hain (gonna, wanna).

## Many accents, one language

| Accent | A few typical features |
| --- | --- |
| American | The r sound is strong. "Water" sounds like "wader". |
| British | The r at the end of a word is often silent. The t is clear. |
| Australian | The vowels are different: "day" can sound like "die". |
| Indian | The t and d are often pronounced with the tongue further back. Stress is often on different syllables. |
| Scottish | The r is rolled. Many vowels are shorter. |

You do not have to remember these. The point is that **differences are normal**, and nobody has the "only correct" accent.

## Connected speech

When people speak fast, they join words, drop sounds and use weak forms.

| Written | Often sounds like | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| going to | gonna | I'm gonna test it. |
| want to | wanna | Do you wanna join? |
| got to / have to | gotta | I gotta go. |
| kind of | kinda | It's kinda slow. |
| did you | didja | Didja see the log? |
| could you | couldja | Couldja share your screen? |
| what do you | whaddaya | Whaddaya think? |
| turn it off | tur-ni-toff | Turn it off. |

You will hear these in videos and meetings. You do not need to say them yourself. **Recognising them is enough.** In writing, always use the full forms.

## How to practise

1. **Choose a clip** and listen to it three times. Then listen to another speaker with a different accent on the same topic.
2. **Turn on English captions** to match sound and spelling.
3. **Listen for the reductions** in the table above, and write down the ones you hear.
4. **Increase variety slowly.** Begin with a clear accent that you understand, and add one new accent every week.
5. **Do not give up** after a difficult video. Come back to it after a week.

## What to do in a real meeting

When you do not understand a person's accent, it is not your fault, and it is not their fault. Use these phrases.

```phrases
Sorry, could you say that again, please?
I didn't catch the last part. Could you repeat it?
Could you speak a little more slowly, please?
Could you write that in the chat?
Just to confirm, you said the deadline is Friday, right?
Thanks, that's clear now.
```

## Your own accent

You do not need to lose your Indian accent. You need to be **clear**: good word stress, clear sounds and a steady pace. People from other countries also ask each other to repeat. This is normal.

## Practice

1. Search for the same topic, for example "daily stand-up" or "code review", in two videos with different accents.
2. Watch both with English captions. Write five phrases that you hear in each.
3. Choose three reductions from the table, and listen for them in the next video you watch.
4. Practise two clarification phrases and use them in the next meeting.

## Recap

- Different accents are normal. Listen to a variety.
- Fast speech joins words: gonna, wanna, gotta, kinda.
- Recognise reductions, but write the full forms.
- Ask politely for a repeat, and confirm the key points.
