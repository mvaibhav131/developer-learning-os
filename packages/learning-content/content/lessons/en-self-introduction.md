"Tell me about yourself" is almost always the first question. It is not a request for your life story. The interviewer wants to know, in about a minute, **who you are professionally, what you can do and why you are here**. Because you can prepare this answer in advance, it is the easiest question to answer well, and a strong start makes you calmer for the rest of the interview.

> **Hinglish mein:** "Myself Rahul, I belong to..." jaisa shuru mat karo. Seedha bolo: "I'm Rahul, a backend developer..." Phir apna kaam, ek achievement, aur kyun yeh job chahiye. 60-90 second, bas.

## The present, past, future formula

| Part | What to say | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Present** | Who you are now: name, role or studies, main skills | 15 seconds |
| **Past** | One or two things you built or achieved, with a result | 30 to 40 seconds |
| **Future** | Why you want this role and what you want to learn | 15 seconds |

About 150 to 200 words. Do not read your CV aloud. They have it in front of them.

## Sample for a fresher

> Hello, I'm Rahul Verma. I have just completed my B.Tech in Computer Science, and I'm looking for a backend developer role. In my final year I built a library management system with Node.js and PostgreSQL, and my classmates used it to borrow books. I also practise data structures regularly, and I enjoy understanding how systems work behind the scenes. I'm excited about this role because your team builds APIs at scale, and I would like to learn from that experience.

## Sample for an experienced developer

> I'm Priya Singh, a backend developer with three years of experience. At my current company I work on a payment service in Java and Spring Boot. Recently I reduced the average response time of our main API by adding a cache and improving two slow queries. I also mentor two junior developers. Now I'm looking for a role where I can work on larger systems and take more ownership, and that's why I'm excited about this position.

These are samples. Use your own real facts. Never invent numbers or companies.

## Useful phrases

```phrases
I'm a backend developer with three years of experience.
I recently graduated in computer science.
At my current company, I work on a payment service.
One thing I'm proud of is reducing the response time of our API.
I enjoy solving problems and learning new tools.
I'm excited about this role because I want to work on larger systems.
```

## Do and do not

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| Start with your name and role | Start with "Myself" or your village and family |
| Mention one real result | List every tool you have ever touched |
| Match your story to the job | Give the same answer for every job |
| Speak slowly and smile | Speak fast and look at the floor |
| End with why you want this job | End with "That's all" and silence |

## Pronunciation checklist

- Say your name slowly and clearly.
- Stress the key words: **backend** developer, **three years**, **payment** service.
- Pause after each sentence. Do not rush.

## Practice

1. Write your answer using the formula. Keep it under 200 words.
2. Read it aloud and time it. The target is 60 to 90 seconds.
3. Record it. Listen once. Fix two things: speed and the first sentence.
4. Say it again without reading. Do this on five different days.
5. Adjust the last two sentences for each company you apply to.

## Recap

- Present, past, future in 60 to 90 seconds.
- Start with "I'm [name], a [role]".
- Include one real achievement, with a result.
- End with why you want this job.
- Practice until you can say it calmly without notes.
