In a good team, people disagree often. You will question a design, disagree with a deadline or push back on a review comment. The goal is to disagree with an idea, not with a person. English has many soft phrases that keep the conversation friendly. Direct sentences such as "You are wrong" or "No, that will not work" can sound rude, even if you do not mean it.

> **Hinglish mein:** Disagree karna theek hai, bas tareeka naram hona chahiye. Formula: pehle point samjho, phir naram tareeke se alag raay do, phir reason batao, aur ek suggestion do.

## A four-step formula

| Step | Purpose | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Acknowledge | Show you listened | I see your point. |
| 2. Soften | Do not sound harsh | I'm not sure that will work, though. |
| 3. Reason | Give a fact or a risk | It might slow down the page because of the extra query. |
| 4. Suggest | Offer another way | What if we use a cache instead? |

Together: "I see your point, but I'm not sure that will work. It might slow down the page because of the extra query. What if we use a cache instead?"

## Phrases for disagreeing

| Strength | Phrase |
| --- | --- |
| Soft | I see your point, but... / That's one option. Another option is... |
| Medium | I'm not sure I agree, because... / I have a slightly different view. |
| Firm but polite | I'm afraid I disagree. / I don't think that will work, because... |
| Asking | Could we consider... ? / What do you think about... ? |

Avoid: "That is wrong." "No, that's not possible." "You don't understand."

## Feedback in code reviews

Make comments about the **code**, use "we" and ask questions.

| Harsh | Polite |
| --- | --- |
| Why did you do this? | Could you help me understand why we chose this approach? |
| This is wrong. | I think this might fail for an empty list. What do you think? |
| Rename this variable. | Could we rename this to `userCount`? It might be clearer. |
| Write tests. | Could we add a test for the error case? |

Useful prefixes: **nit:** (a small, optional point), **question:**, **suggestion:**, **blocking:** (must be fixed before merge).

## Receiving feedback

```phrases
Thanks for the feedback. I'll update it today.
Good catch. I'll fix that.
I hadn't thought of that. Thanks for pointing it out.
Could you explain that a little more?
I see your point, but I chose this because it is simpler. What do you think?
I understand. Let's try your option and compare the results.
```

## Say it aloud

```phrases
I see your point, but I'm not sure that will work.
It might slow down the page because of the extra query.
What if we use a cache instead?
I'm afraid I disagree, but I'm happy to look at the data together.
Could we discuss this after the meeting?
```

## Tone tips

1. Say **we**, not you. "Could we add a test?" not "You forgot the test."
2. Use words such as **might**, **maybe**, **I think**. They soften your message.
3. Ask a question. "What do you think?" invites a discussion.
4. Give a **reason**. A disagreement without a reason sounds like opinion.
5. Keep your voice calm and your speed steady.

## Practice

Rewrite these as polite disagreements.

1. "Your plan is wrong." Possible answer: "I see your point, but I'm not sure this plan will work. Could we look at the risks?"
2. "We cannot finish by Friday." Possible answer: "I'm afraid Friday might be difficult because the testing is not finished. Could we move the demo to Monday?"

## Recap

- Formula: acknowledge, soften, give a reason, suggest.
- Disagree with the idea, not the person. Use we and questions.
- In reviews, use prefixes such as nit and question, and make suggestions.
- When you get feedback, say thanks, and answer calmly.
