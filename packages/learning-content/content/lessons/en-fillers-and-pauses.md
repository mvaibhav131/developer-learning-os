Everybody freezes sometimes, even native speakers. The problem is not the pause. The problem is what you do in it. Many speakers fill every second with "um", "uh", "like" or "basically", and the listener starts to hear the fillers more than the message. A planned pause sounds calm and confident. This lesson gives you phrases for when you need time and a method to reduce fillers.

> **Hinglish mein:** Chup rehna galat nahi hai. "Um, uh" ki jagah ek chhota pause lo, ya koi ready phrase bolo jaise "Let me think for a second". Isse tum confident lagte ho.

## Fillers: when they are fine and when they are too much

| Filler | Comment |
| --- | --- |
| um, uh | Natural in small amounts. Too many make you sound unsure. |
| like | Do not use it as a comma. "It's, like, slow, like, today." |
| you know | Fine now and then in casual talk. Avoid in presentations. |
| basically, actually | Useful words, but not in every sentence. |
| so... | Good to start a sentence. Not to fill the silence for several seconds. |

## Replace the filler with a pause

1. Notice your own filler. Record one minute of speaking and count the fillers.
2. When you feel "um" coming, **close your mouth and take a breath** instead.
3. Finish the sentence, **pause for one second**, then start the next.
4. Slow down a little. Fillers appear more when you speak too fast.

## Phrases to buy time

```phrases
Let me think about that for a second.
That's a good question. Give me a moment.
Let me put this in a simple way.
What I mean is, the build was slow because of the cache.
Sorry, let me say that again.
Can I come back to that in a minute?
The word I'm looking for is "deployment".
To put it another way, the system was not ready.
```

## When your mind goes blank

| Step | What you say or do |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Stay calm | Take one slow breath. Nobody notices a two-second pause. |
| 2. Say a buying-time phrase | "Let me think about that for a second." |
| 3. Give one simple point | "The main problem was the slow query." |
| 4. Add one reason or example | "For example, it took ten seconds with a large table." |
| 5. End clearly | "That's the main point." |

## A simple structure: point, reason, example

When you answer any question, use three short steps.

- **Point:** "I prefer to use a cache."
- **Reason:** "Because it makes the page faster."
- **Example:** "For example, our home page went from three seconds to one."

This structure gives you something to say, so you are less likely to freeze.

## Do not over-apologise

Phrases such as "Sorry, my English is weak" make the listener focus on mistakes. One calm sentence is enough: "Let me say that again." Then continue.

## Practice

1. Choose a topic: your last project, your daily routine or your favourite tool.
2. Speak for 60 seconds. Record it.
3. Listen and count the fillers. Write the number.
4. Speak again on the same topic, with calm pauses. Count again.
5. Repeat for a week and watch the number go down.

## Recap

- A pause is better than um and uh. Breathe and continue.
- Learn buying-time phrases like "Let me think for a second".
- Use point, reason, example to give an answer when you are nervous.
- Do not over-apologise. One calm sentence, then continue.
