Watching English videos is not the same as learning English from videos. If you watch with Hindi subtitles, or only let the video play while you do something else, you hear English, but your brain does not work. Real listening practice needs **attention**, **a clear goal** and **repetition**. The good news is that it does not need a long time. Twenty focused minutes with a good method are better than two hours of passive watching.

> **Hinglish mein:** Sirf video chalu rakhna practice nahi hai. Ek chhota clip lo, teen baar alag tareeke se dekho: pehle bina subtitle ke samjho, phir English subtitle ke saath, phir dobara bina subtitle ke. Naye phrases likhte jao.

## Choose the right video

- **Slightly above your level.** You should understand about 70 percent. If you understand 100 percent, it is too easy. If you understand 30 percent, it is too hard.
- **Short:** two to five minutes.
- **Clear audio** and **English captions** if possible.
- **A topic you care about:** developer stand-ups, tech talks, interviews, product demos.

## The three-pass method

| Pass | What to do | Subtitles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Gist | Watch once. Write one sentence: what is the main idea? | Off |
| 2. Details | Watch again with English captions. Pause to write five new words or phrases. | English |
| 3. Check | Watch again. How much more do you understand now? | Off |

Then use one or two of the phrases in your speaking practice or in a message.

## Dictation: the strongest exercise

1. Choose a clear 20 to 30 second part.
2. Play one sentence, pause, and **write exactly what you hear**.
3. Repeat the sentence up to five times, then compare with the captions.
4. Mark the words you missed. Why did you miss them? Fast speech? A word you did not know? A reduced sound?

## A 20-minute routine

| Minutes | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 5 | Pass 1: watch the clip without subtitles. Write the main idea. |
| 5 to 12 | Pass 2: with captions. Write five new phrases. |
| 12 to 17 | Dictation of one short part. |
| 17 to 20 | Pass 3, then say two new phrases aloud. |

## Tools in the video player

- **Speed:** 0.75x for hard parts, 1x for normal practice.
- **Captions:** use English captions, not Hindi ones, for your second pass.
- **Repeat:** replay a short part again and again.
- **Transcript:** some videos show a transcript. Use it after the first listen.

## Types of listening

| Type | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intensive | One short clip, studied carefully | The three-pass method, dictation |
| Extensive | Many easy videos, for the general idea | A tech podcast during travel |

Do both: intensive on weekdays, extensive on weekends.

## Phrases to collect while you listen

```phrases
Let's get started with a quick update.
I'll walk you through the main changes.
Does that make sense so far?
Let me share my screen.
Any questions before we move on?
That's all from my side.
```

## Honest expectations

- Listening improves with regular practice. You may not notice it in a week.
- Understanding every word is not the goal. Understanding the main idea and the key words is.
- Passive background listening can help you get used to the sound, but it does not replace focused practice.

## Recap

- Choose short, clear clips slightly above your level.
- Use three passes: no captions, English captions, no captions again.
- Dictation shows exactly what you miss.
- Twenty focused minutes a day beat hours of passive watching.
